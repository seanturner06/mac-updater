Updates without the babysitting

01

It checks every source at once

Homebrew, casks, the App Store, and your global npm packages — scanned in parallel the moment you open it. The menu-bar icon tells you the count at a glance.

02

You choose what updates

Tick the ones you want, or update everything. Skip a version you're not ready for. Prefer to just be told? Flip on notify-only and Freshet never touches a thing.

03

It runs and shows its work

Real Homebrew output, surfaced cleanly — so when something needs your attention, you see exactly what and why instead of a silent failure.

Built for people who actually read their changelogs

Your whole dev toolchain

The only updater that tracks your global npm packages alongside your apps. typescript, pnpm, the CLI tools you forget you installed — all current, all in one place.

Knows when the signer changes

Freshet flags any app suddenly signed by a new developer certificate — the quiet signal of a hijacked update. Nobody else surfaces this. It's the difference between updating and getting owned.

Tell me, don't touch it

Notify-only mode for the security-conscious: see what's outdated without ever handing a tool the keys to install. Your Mac, your call.

Bring loose apps into Homebrew

Guided adoption finds apps you installed by hand and pulls them under Homebrew — so they update with everything else from then on.

One-click rollback soon

Freshet snapshots an app before it swaps it. A bad update is one click away from being undone — the safety net auto-updaters never give you.

Calm by design

No dashboard of a thousand options. No 150 MB of Electron sitting in your menu bar. A small, quiet tool that does one thing and gets out of your way.

The version database is someone else's problem — on purpose.

The last great Mac updater died maintaining a private catalog of a hundred thousand apps by hand. Freshet doesn't. It leans on the registries the community already keeps current — Homebrew Cask, the App Store, npm — so it never goes stale and never asks you to trust a catalog you can't see. The core is open source, the binary is notarized and signed, and nothing about your machine leaves it.
