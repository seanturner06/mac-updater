WHY IT'S FASTER — and where the new floor is
  - The 24 formulae (164 MB) fetched in ONE process in 8.82 s: brew loaded metadata
    once and downloaded the long tail concurrently (16-way), instead of paying
    ~1 s of Ruby/metadata startup per token across 24+ separate processes.
  - The two casks (299 MB = 65% of bytes) fetched in 11.10 s in a second process.
    That batch is now the CRITICAL PATH — the wall-clock (11.11 s) equals the cask
    batch, which ran concurrently with the formula batch (8.82 s).
  - Remaining time is genuine network transfer, not overhead: 299 MB / 11.1 s ≈
    27 MB/s for the casks, and 41.7 MB/s aggregate across both kind-threads
    (~350 Mbit/s) — the link / CDN ceiling for this machine right now, not a
    concurrency defect. Nothing more to squeeze without more/faster streams on the
    two big casks (which is up to the upstream CDN).

TAKEAWAY
  The original 17.80 s was inflated by per-item `brew fetch` startup plus the
  harness's redundant `brew --cache` probe. The shipped prefetch_selected()
  (batched `brew fetch` per kind) removes both and runs at the network floor.
